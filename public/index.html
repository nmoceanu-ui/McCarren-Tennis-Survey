Tennis for all of Brooklyn.

New York hosts the biggest tennis tournament on earth, and it's the hardest place to play tennis. Brooklyn Challenger is working on three things: court time you can count on, real hours for kids, and courts nobody has fixed in years.

    Court time you can count on

    Right now most people walk to a court and hope it's free. We're asking NYC Parks to put more Brooklyn courts on online booking, keep the yearly permit price the same, and publish when courts are free so anyone can see it. Until then, players can check in on our map.

    Tap the clock

    Kids get the courts after school

    Kids are in school all day. We're asking for weekday kids' hours from 4 to 6 pm, when juniors get the courts first.

    Tap to resurface it

    Bring forgotten courts back

    Of the 257 courts we mapped, 87 look like they need resurfacing and 6 look abandoned. We want every one of them playable.

    Project 1

    First up: McCarren Park

    Next to McCarren's seven courts sits about 78,000 square feet of asphalt. NYC Parks is designing it now, from June 2026 to July 2027, with a stormwater tank planned underneath. At Parks' May workshop, more tennis courts were among the most-requested uses.

    • 9NYC Parks courts for about 325,000 people in North Brooklyn
    • 7of those 9 are paid courts from October to April, $50 to $100 an hour
    • +31kpeople added in Community District 1 from 2010 to 2020, and no courts added

    In 2026 the McCarren Tennis Association lit all seven courts with $300,000 raised from neighbors and USTA grants.

    Tap the lot to see our idea

    Sketch, not to scale and not a Parks design. Our idea: resurface the seven, add four public courts with a show court, plus lawn, a flex pad and the dog run. It is one option we're bringing to Parks and Community Board 1.

    The ten-year goal

    A New York kid in the US Open quarterfinals

    The US Open is played in Queens every summer. By 2037 we want one of its quarterfinalists to be a player who learned the game on New York's public courts.

    That starts small: courts a kid can get on, after-school hours, junior tournaments close to home, and a pro event in Brooklyn they can watch.
